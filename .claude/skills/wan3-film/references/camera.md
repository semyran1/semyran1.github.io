# Camera - vocabulary, movement, motivation

Marks as in `wan3-core/references/provenance.md`. Ids are official handbook prompts
(`wan3-core/references/handbook-prompts.md`). Read this whole file before writing any camera line.

Contents: 1 The rule - 2 Shot size and angle - 3 Movements and what they say - 4 Chaining and
the continuous take - 5 Handheld and camera character - 6 Locked-off - 7 Speed, direction,
displacement - 8 Whip, snap, orbit, FPV, aerial - 9 Reaction time - 10 Slow motion and freezes

## 1. The rule

One main camera treatment per shot, named, with subject and both ends (`wan3-film` §2). Alibaba's
guide: plain wording works - *push in, pull out, orbit, handheld follow* `[DOC]` (excerpt) - and each
move is chosen for what it says; the "every shot" requirement is `[DOC-2]`. "Cinematic" is not a treatment.

## 2. Shot size and angle `[DOC]`

Alibaba's slot lists *shot size* and *camera angle* separately from *lens* and *camera movement*.
Use the standard terms and never combine contradictory ones in one shot (a "close-up" and a
"wide shot" at once is resolved arbitrarily `[FIELD]`).

| Size | Use | Handbook form |
|---|---|---|
| Extreme wide / long | smallness, world | `extreme long shot, the protagonist's figure appearing exceptionally small` (009) |
| Wide / panorama | where we are, who is in it | `Panorama of a small train station platform on a rainy night` (013) |
| Medium / medium-long | action and body | `a medium-long establishing shot` (008) |
| Medium close-up | speech, listening | `frontal medium close-up` (001), `Medium close-up with a slight high angle` (011) |
| Close-up | the detail that carries the beat | `Close-up of hands, the girl hands a folded note` (013) |
| Extreme close-up | a thing becoming the world | `Extreme close-up, on the edge of a morning lotus leaf, a water droplet` (064) |
| Over-the-shoulder | confrontation, listening | `Over-the-shoulder shot from behind the boy` (013) |
| POV | subjective | `first-person POV, simulating the use of a small action camera` (028) |

Angles: eye-level, low (power, looming), high (smallness, surveillance), top-down, profile,
frontal-symmetrical (017: `strictly symmetrical left and right`). A pair that works: size for
the information, angle for the attitude, one sentence each.

## 3. Movements and what they say `[DOC]` (the intent list) `[CRAFT]` (the rest)

| Move | Say it as | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Push-in | `the camera slowly pushes in from a medium shot to her eyes` | intimacy, decision, tension; reserve for a beat that earns it |
| Pull-out / pull-back | `the camera slowly pulls back to a medium-long shot, revealing ...` | reveal, release, isolation (061) |
| Pan / whip pan | `pans right to a close-up of the idol's face` (003) | follow attention; whip pan for energy |
| Tilt | `extremely slow tilt up to eye level during the monologue` (011) | status change, reveal of scale |
| Track / follow | `the camera smoothly tracking backward` (002), `follows closely along the ground like a skateboarder` (004) | company with the subject; backward tracking toward the camera is the chase grammar |
| Orbit | `executes a 180-degree horizontal orbit` (004), `The camera orbits the swordsman rapidly` (062) | emphasis, a character at the centre of events |
| Crane / aerial | `a diving descent from high altitude, returning to the ground` (004) | scale, landing |
| Dolly / parallax | `camera slowly pans + slight press` (019), parallax layering | depth, material |
| Zoom / snap zoom | `rapid zoom push-in` (004) | a punch; use sparingly |
| Handheld | `a slight handheld breathing sensation and subtle shake` (001) | presence, documentary pressure |
| Fixed | `fixed stable camera position` (043) | stillness, a performance or a showcase |

## 4. Chaining and the continuous take `[DOC]` `[CRAFT]`

A one-take may chain moves in written order, each with subject and end state, and carries its
no-cut clause at each move (003, 004, 064; `wan3-core` long-form §3). Official chains read like
routes: 004 goes `backward pull-out and sink to ground level` -> `follows along the ground` ->
`parabolic lift` -> `pushes forward ... crosshair-style` -> `180-degree horizontal orbit` ->
`diving descent` -> `lands` -> `rapid zoom push-in, freezing`. Each is one clause; each hands
over to the next at a named object (the ramp, the billboard, the street). **Route the camera
through objects, not through directions.**

Montage is requested by saying montage (`pure jump cut editing logic`, 037), not by counting
moves. Cutting grammar for coverage (axis, shot/reverse shot) is `[CRAFT]` from
`seedance-25-coverage` and transfers: keep the axis, cut on a completed action.

## 5. Handheld and camera character `[DOC]`

028 is the one prompt in the corpus that describes a camera as a person. Copy its method:

- the source of the shake: `The shake must come from breathing, footsteps, turning inertia, and
  startled reactions, not regular vibration, nor constant high-frequency random shaking`
- the idle state: `When observing statically, there are also small breathing fluctuations and
  left-right weight shifts`
- the coupling: `When raising an arm, the camera slightly offsets in the opposite direction`
- the stop: `When turning the lens, it accelerates first, then decelerates, with a very small
  overshoot and rebound at the stopping position`

Handheld is small instability tied to an operator's breath and weight, never digital jitter. State
the subject followed and the amount of shake.

## 6. Locked-off `[FIELD]` `[CRAFT]`

Say it (`fixed shot`) or Wan moves the camera. Then say what stays: `the door frame, the table edge
and the window stay at the same place in frame for the whole shot`. A fixed camera with a
performing subject (001: `Although the shot is static, it carries a slight handheld breathing
sensation`) is allowed: name both.

## 7. Speed, direction, displacement

Speed words matter and are cheap: *slowly, sharply, rapidly, extremely slow, swiftly* (Alibaba's
motion guidance `[FIELD]`; handbook: `extremely slow tilt up`, `quickly pans`). For direction,
write the displacement in frame: which subject moves toward which edge, what grows, what shrinks,
what fills the vacated space. "The ground drops away" is a concept; "the city lights sink toward
the bottom edge, shrinking, and the vacated upper frame fills with black sky" is an instruction.
`<primary subject> stays fully in frame` is a checkable end state.

## 8. Whip, snap, orbit, FPV, aerial

- **Whip pan** is the cleanest hard transition: `fast whip pans, snap zooms` (038) are the
  grammar of the official chase film. The cut lands in the blur.
- **FPV** `[DOC]` (025): `exaggerated FPV camera movement`, with route, speed and turns: `flying
  low-altitude along the planet's surface ... continuously banks and rolls`.
- **Orbit**: state the arc (`180-degree`), the subject at the centre and the speed. A fast orbit
  smears grain.
- **Aerial / drone**: height, direction, area revealed. Declined by restrained styles (Fincher).
- **High-speed photography**: `High-speed photography (1000fps slow motion)` (037) for a moment
  that must read crisp (spray, sparks, hair).

## 9. Reaction time `[DOC]`

`Hand movements occur first, and the camera follows with about a 0.1-second delay, creating a
realistic reaction time`; `After movement stops, autofocus needs about 0.2 seconds to re-lock`
(028). One sentence like this turns a gimbal glide into an operator. Use when realism of the
*operator* matters; skip in animated or stylized work.

## 10. Slow motion and freezes `[DOC]`

Freezes close official shots on purpose (`The frame finally freezes on this expression`, 003;
`freezing into a perfect movie poster composition`, 064). A designed last frame is also insurance:
if the model drops the final beat (a reported weak spot `[FIELD]`), a freeze costs nothing.
Slow motion is a treatment, not a cure for a weak action: name its trigger (in 009 the scene
enters slow motion at the moment the girl leaps).

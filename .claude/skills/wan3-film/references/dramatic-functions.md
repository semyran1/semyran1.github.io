# Dramatic functions - what a shot is for, and how Wan builds it

Marks as in `wan3-core/references/provenance.md`. The six functions and the 2-3-techniques-per-clip
discipline come from the `cinema-director-ru` workflow (Establish, Reveal, Escalate, Break, Climax,
Resolve); what is added here is how Wan 3 builds each one: the camera treatment is named, the cut
state is declared, and **sound is written in**, because Wan generates it in the same pass. All
`[CRAFT]` - untested on Wan; examples cite the official handbook where it shows the same move.
Read this whole file when planning a multi-shot scene; pick **one function per shot, two or three
techniques per clip, never more** (accents blur).

Contents: 1 Establish - 2 Reveal - 3 Escalate - 4 Break - 5 Climax - 6 Resolve - 7 Order and
transitions

## 1. Establish - where, who, what world, without exposition

- **Environment before person.** The camera holds a detail of the place; the subject enters at
  the frame edge and does not take the centre. Fixed shot or the slowest push. Handbook: 013 opens
  on a panorama of the platform before the girl is a face.
- **Descent into the space.** Extreme high angle, an imperceptibly slow descent; the subject
  appears small, as part of the environment. Wan: `slow top-down descent over N seconds`; keep it slow - a fast
  descent loses the sense of scale and the control of the frame.
- **Sound before picture.** The first 1-2 s carry ambience only (rain on a roof, a hum): write the
  bed first, the subject later (008 states its sound bed: `night insect chirps, faint water
  sounds, low-frequency resonance`).
- Cut state: none (opening). Anti-pattern: an establishing shot that already contains the plot.

## 2. Reveal - the thing is shown, the audience understands

- **Rack focus reveal**: both planes named, each plane's change (`wan3-film` optics).
- **Subject enters the light**: the figure is a silhouette, then the key arrives on the face
  (011 low-key with a moonlit hard side light).
- **Reflection as first appearance**: a mirror, a puddle, a window.
- **Trigger before reaction**: show what is seen before the face that sees it, then cut or move
  to the face; do not push into a face-only close-up first.
- Sound: a cue **just before** the visual (a latch, a breath); in the handbook 021 the kitten
  whimper `<kitten whimper>` precedes the door opening.
- Cut state: a motivated cut on a completed action, or none.

## 3. Escalate - pressure rises, nothing explodes yet

- **The invisible push-in**: the camera advances so slowly the frame tightens without being
  noticed; one per scene, started when a character stops talking and commits.
- **Narrowing depth of field**: the world around the subject softens as the problem tightens.
- **Layering sound**: a second, third element enters at intervals (006: low drone -> drum pulses
  -> strings -> brass). Write the arc over time, not "tense music".
- Quantified rhythm helps here: `the falling speed of bamboo leaves increases` (062 shot 2).
- Cut state: no cut or slowly shortening ranges. Anti-pattern: speed up everything; tension is
  content and stillness, not tempo.

## 4. Break - the pattern stops

- **Freeze at impact**: `slow motion to freeze frame` (062 shot 5): the blade stops beside the
  neck. Name what freezes.
- **Close-up of stillness**: the face, the hand, after the event.
- **The space after the subject leaves**: hold the empty frame for a beat.
- Sound: cut it. A drop to silence or a single room tone reads as a break more than any effect.
- Cut state: hard cut on the beat or none; either way declared.

## 5. Climax - the one moment the camera finally commits

- **The first and only handheld**: a restrained film breaks its own grammar once, at the peak.
- **Sound peaks and cuts** (see Break): peak, then silence; or the voice arrives at a stated
  second (064: `From the 8th second ... a gentle female voice slowly recites`).
- **POV**: the camera becomes the subject.
- Budget: the climax belongs in the middle third of a 30 s clip, not the last 5 s (a reported weak
  spot is the dropped closing beat `[FIELD]`); the landing after it is cheap.

## 6. Resolve - the effect of the event

- **Pull away from the subject** to a wider frame; the world continues.
- **A practical stabilizes**: a flickering light settles, rain weakens (013: `the rain gradually
  weakens`).
- **The empty space with a trace**: the footprint, the note left behind.
- End on a **designed last frame** (freeze, fade, poster composition: 003, 064): it survives
  whatever the model does to the last second.

## 7. Order and transitions

Typical arcs: Establish -> Reveal -> Escalate -> Climax -> Resolve (a 15-30 s story); Establish ->
Escalate -> Break -> Resolve (a quiet one). Between functions choose one cut state per boundary
(`wan3-core` long-form §3): environment -> subject usually no cut; Escalate -> Break a hard cut or
a freeze; Climax -> Resolve a pull-away without a cut. For a scene with one camera and no cuts,
the functions become **phases of one move**: the same push-in is Escalate in second 6 and Break
in second 12 when the sound drops.
